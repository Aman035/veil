Why a VPN or Tor Browser isn't enough

A VPN gives all your accounts one IP, so they still look like one person. Tor Browser isolates by site, not by account, so two wallets on the same dapp share an exit, and many providers block it outright. Veil gives each account its own circuit.

How it works

One intercept, a circuit per account, nothing silently direct.

Veil sits between the page and its RPC provider. No change to the dapp, no new endpoint - it catches the reads the app already makes and carries each one over Tor, hash-pinned and sandboxed.

  1. 1

    Catch the read in the page

    At document_start, before the dapp's own scripts run, Veil wraps window.fetch and XMLHttpRequest. Every JSON-RPC read goes through one of those, so a single hook covers viem, ethers v5/v6 and raw XHR with no per-app integration. The rare read over a WebSocket or Web Worker isn't wrapped yet, a negligible slice of traffic.

  2. 2

    Key it to one account, blind the id

    Veil reads which account the request is for - from the params, or from inside eth_call calldata, ignoring the zero address - and gives it an anon-rpc isolation key. The same account always rides the same Tor circuit; two accounts never share one. The JSON-RPC id is blinded (viem numbers requests from one global counter) so a provider can't stitch routes back together by id.

  3. 3

    Send it over Tor, sandboxed

    The read is handed to the anon-rpc harness: a null-origin, sandboxed iframe running tor-js, a full Tor client compiled to WebAssembly. Its code is fetched at runtime but hash-pinned on-chain and verified before it runs, with no access to your keys, DOM or cookies. It builds a circuit through a blind KPS gateway that relays only encrypted bytes - never your traffic or destination - and reaches the provider from a Tor exit unique to that account.

  4. 4

    Re-route around a block, only after a real one

    If a provider refuses Tor (Aave's blocked all 16 of our test reads), Veil re-sends standard reads to a Tor-friendly provider on the same chain - but only after a real block, and only for endpoints it knows are a single standard RPC. A multichain gateway is never blindly re-routed (that would break it); it falls to your per-site choice.

  5. 5

    Never silently direct

    A read that can't be made private is blocked, or asks you - per site - before any direct connection. Nothing leaves in the clear, and nothing leaks while the first circuit is still building.
