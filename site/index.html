Why it's different

Privacy shouldn't wait for apps to adopt it.

The real fix is apps integrating anon-rpc, but only a couple have, all opt-in, and until the rest do, you are the one who pays, with every read exposed. Veil moves the decision to you: turn it on once and every wallet and dapp you already use is private today, with nothing for them to change.

The old way: app by app

Each dapp and wallet has to integrate privacy itself. Only a couple have, all opt-in. You wait, app by app, and most never do.

With Veil: your decision

Turn it on once. Every wallet and dapp you already use is private, per account, with zero changes from them.

How it works

One route per account. Re-routes around blocks. Never silently direct.

Each account gets its own Tor circuit and exit, so a provider sees three strangers instead of one person. Account-agnostic reads share a neutral route.

Aave's provider rejects Tor outright. Veil re-routes standard reads to a Tor-friendly provider on the same chain - only after a real block, never blindly.

A route per account

Veil reads the account from the request - even inside the call data - and gives it its own circuit. Request IDs are blinded so a counter can't stitch routes back together.

Works through blocks

When a provider refuses Tor, Veil re-sends the read through a Tor-friendly provider on the same chain. Your apps keep working - privately.

Never silently direct

If a read can't be made private, Veil asks you - per site - before any direct connection. Nothing leaks while Tor connects.

Honest about privacy

What it hides - and what it doesn't.

Privacy you don't actually have is worse than none. Here's the exact line - the same one we show inside the product.

Hidden from providers
  • Your IP address
    On every dapp read, and on your local tools through the desktop app.
  • That your accounts belong together
    For RPC traffic, each account gets its own route, so providers see unrelated strangers.
Still visible
  • The addresses you query
    Providers still see which addresses you read. Concealing that requires private information retrieval (PIR), which is beyond the scope of this project.
  • Your wallet's multi-account APIs
    Some wallet services request every account in a single call. Veil hides your IP on that traffic, but the accounts remain linked at the application layer. The desktop app can split these requests per account, disabled by default.
  • That you use Tor
    The Tor gateway can see that you use Tor, but not which sites you visit or what you read.
